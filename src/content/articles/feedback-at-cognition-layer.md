Most educational technology closes the loop at the *outcome* layer: a score, a
grade, a completion. That's the easiest signal to capture and the least useful
one to act on.

The harder, more valuable place to intervene is the **cognition layer** — the
reasoning as it happens, before it collapses into a right-or-wrong answer.

## Why the outcome layer disappoints

An outcome tells you *that* something went wrong, not *where*. Two students can
miss the same question for entirely different reasons, and a score erases that
difference.

## What the cognition layer asks of us

It asks us to model the learner's state — what they can do, what they're
reaching for, where the reasoning bends. That's a much bigger commitment than a
gradebook, and it's the whole point.
