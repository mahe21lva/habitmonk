title: "Arriving at clarity"
date: "2025-08"
topic: work
stage: forming
excerpt: "The point where thinking encounters something it hasn't resolved yet."
---

I am trying to describe something that happens while learning.

Suppose that while practicing a problem set, solving a question, or writing a piece, we have a few hesitations.\
Some missing clarity.\
We uncover something that was unknown to us.

There is a point where something happens, but I am struggling to find the right word for that point.

Is it a struggle?\
Is it discomfort?\
Is it uncertainty?\
Maybe all three belong to the same basket.\
I want a word for that basket.\
The point where thinking encounters something it hasn't resolved yet.

Our ability to think clearly doesn't just pop out coherently from our head.\
Most often, we struggle through this discomfort.\
We try a variety of things before we resolve this tension and move ahead.

So perhaps clarity needs a lot of questioning and reasoning.\
A principle of arriving at clarity could be the Socratic method:

Ask why at each stage.\
Uncover the struggles and discomfort associated with it.\
Understand the “why” behind each struggle.\
Resolve the tension.\
Combine the pieces.\
Become coherent.\
Move ahead.

Maybe that's all the basis we need.\
The discipline to follow this rule and apply it at every step of our System 2 thinking.

Now, what does this mean for a student?

Writing for clarity before writing with clarity.\
A student needs to write while they are still figuring things out.\
Writing for clarity questions what a student actually knows.

But we know that metacognition at that age is difficult to attain for everyone.\
So perhaps we need a tool.\
A framework that helps a student point out what their discomforts are during practice.\
Let's call it a note-taking system.

A student answering a question could mark the exact spot where the discomfort happens.\
Maybe simply put a “No” at that point.\
Then move forward.\
Even if they have resolved the tension at that place, keep going.\
At the end of the answer, simply write down what they struggled with.

If we are already scoring their answers, what if we also give them points for understanding what they know?\
What if the first attempt at each question is not just an attempt to get the answer right?\
What if it is an attempt to expose the thinking?\
Then we get a better view of how a student thinks.

Of course, there are better note-taking methods once the tension is resolved.\
Cornell notes when explaining a concept.\
Mind maps when we are able to make connections between concepts.\
Zettelkasten when we want to build a network of ideas.\
These forms are useful once we reach some level of clarity.

But perhaps they are not the right tool for Level 0.\
At Level 0, the actual struggle is happening.\
A much simpler method may be required.

There is no rush to be coherent.\
Just point out the discomfort.\
Keep going.\
Come back to it.\
Understand why it was uncomfortable.\
Resolve it.\
Then write it down properly.

So perhaps, borrowing from Zettelkasten, we need two notebooks for each subject we learn.\
Thought-forming notes.\
And thought-clarified notes.\
Thought-forming notes are useful for arriving at clarity.\
Thought-clarified notes are useful once clarity has arrived.

Maybe the first notebook shouldn't look good.\
Maybe it shouldn't even make sense to anyone else.\
It is a record of thinking before the thinking has become clear.

And perhaps that is precisely what we are missing.\
We are very good at recording what students know.\
We are much less good at recording where they were uncertain while learning it.

Maybe every student's first attempt should leave behind a map of that uncertainty.\
I'm not sure yet.\
That's probably the point.
